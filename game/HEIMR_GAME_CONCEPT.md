# HEIMR — Game Concept

## Founder direction

Build a companion adventure to the HEIMR book with Corey, Maverick, and the young dragon HEIMR as the playable party. It should feel like stepping into the book together: curious, funny, emotionally real, and full of places worth exploring.

The camera should use a readable 3/4 top-down view. Walking up the screen moves farther into the scene; foreground objects can overlap the characters to suggest depth. Use layered pixel art, expressive animation, rich little environments, and camera transitions to create a 3D feeling with a carefully authored 2D world. This is a visual reference to the broad perspective and exploratory feel of classic 16-bit adventures, including *A Link to the Past*; all characters, maps, puzzles, sound, and art must be original Studio LaDuke work.

## Story anchors

- Corey and Maverick are distinct playable characters, not generic stand-ins.
- HEIMR is the dragon hatchling at the center of the book's shard-memory story.
- The shard creates discoveries and consequences. It should not act as a generic key that erases the emotional cost from the story.
- Preserve the book's core places and moments as they are settled in the manuscript; do not invent a game plot that contradicts the book.
- Build a welcoming opening chapter first. Let Corey and Maverick explore together, notice the strange shard, and encounter HEIMR in a playable scene before expanding the world.

## Play pattern

1. Explore compact, hand-built areas with short routes, useful landmarks, and secrets.
2. Talk and interact with people, animals, objects, and environmental clues.
3. Swap between Corey, Maverick, and HEIMR when their different size, movement, or shard abilities matter.
4. Solve physical puzzles using position, light, sound, objects, and the shard's memory effects.
5. Return to known places with new abilities and notice that the world and relationships have changed.

## Art and sound

- Use original, expressive sprite art with readable silhouettes and directional walk cycles.
- Give the world clear near, middle, and far layers; make trees, doorways, bridges, and ledges communicate depth.
- Keep the palette grounded in the book's Pacific Northwest atmosphere while allowing the shard and dragon magic to bring color forward.
- Compose an original score with small location themes and restrained motifs for Corey, Maverick, HEIMR, and the shard.
- Keep music and controls usable on touch devices and let players stop safely at any time.

## First playable target

Create one polished 10–15 minute chapter before expanding. It needs a clear start, a welcoming space, a short exterior route, a meaningful discovery, one memorable puzzle, a save/checkpoint, and a satisfying chapter ending. Test movement and interaction on a Chromebook and phone before writing more content.

## Fort cartridge shelf

The Fort shelf is a growing collection of real Studio LaDuke games. A cartridge is labeled **Play now** only when its game is implemented and tested. HEIMR can appear as a clearly marked upcoming cartridge while it is in development; it must not promise a playable game before one exists.
